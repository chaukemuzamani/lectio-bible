Word details panel

Using it. Tap a verse number to highlight or copy the verse. Highlights are saved to your account, follow the verse into every version, and are listed under Highlights above. ← and → turn chapters, / opens search. Search accepts words, a reference, or a Strong's number such as H430 or G26.

Word links. Pointing at a word in either column lights up the matching word in the other. The KJV, the Hebrew Old Testament and the four Greek New Testaments are hand-tagged with Strong's numbers, so their links are exact. Every other version, Russian and Shona included, is matched statistically: each word is paired with the Hebrew or Greek word it most often appears with across the whole Bible. English words are first matched against the English gloss of each Hebrew or Greek word in the verse. Rarer words are then matched by weaker associations, and finally by position in the verse. Tested against the KJV's hand tags, almost every content word gets a match (96%), and about 83% of matches are right. Those matches are marked "≈ approximate" in the details strip. Common small words (the, of, and; и, в, на) are not linked.

Words of Jesus. The KJV and the WEB (Yahweh edition) mark Jesus' words in their own text. In every other version the red runs exactly from the opening to the closing quotation mark of what Jesus says, so the narrator's words around it ("He looked at him and said") stay black. Which quotations are his comes from the WEB; in the Greek texts each word is red when the KJV word with the same Strong's number is. Turn it off under Words of Jesus in red.

Quotation marks. The KJV, ASV, Darby, Webster, UKJV, Geneva, Douay-Rheims, Tyndale, BBE, Brenton, the Synodal, Church Slavonic and Makarius print no quotation marks, so Lectio adds them where someone speaks, following the speech in the WEB matched word by word (Russian through the Hebrew and Greek words behind each word, with «…» and „…“). Where a match is unsure, no mark is added. YLT, JPS and NHEB-ME print straight marks, shown as typographic ones. The added marks can be turned off under Quotation marks.

Headings. Section headings and paragraphs follow the BSB in every version (in Russian for the Synodal, Church Slavonic and Makarius), with links to parallel passages. Psalm titles are set apart from verse 1.

Footnotes. The KJV translators' margin notes, and the footnotes of the BSB and the WEB (Yahweh edition), appear as red letters in the text and in a list below each chapter.

Greek editions. NA28, SBL, Textus Receptus and Byzantine each use that edition's choice of words, drawn from the STEPBible amalgamated text. Spelling and word order follow the STEPBible base text, so they can differ slightly from the printed editions.

Numbering. Each version keeps its own chapter and verse numbers: the Synodal and Church Slavonic use Septuagint psalm numbers (Psalm 23 is 22) and count psalm titles as verses, JPS follows the Hebrew Bible (Malachi 4 is Malachi 3:19–24), Douay-Rheims the Vulgate and Brenton the Septuagint. Compare lines the two versions up verse by verse and shows the other version's own verse numbers where they differ. Highlights stay on the same verse in every version.
